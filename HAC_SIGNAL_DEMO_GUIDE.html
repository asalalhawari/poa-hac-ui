02. Domain Fundamentals: HAC vs. POA

The core distinction that determines medical liability, clinical quality rating, and claim reimbursement.

HAC (HOSPITAL-ACQUIRED COMPLICATION)

In-Stay Quality Breakdown

An adverse physical injury, infection, or physiologic event that was absent upon emergency or elective admission, but occurred while the patient was hospitalized.

  • Surgical site dehiscence & deep space MRSA infections
  • Indwelling Foley catheter-associated urinary tract infections (CAUTI)
  • Stage 2 to 4 sacral decubitus ulcers
  • Hospital-acquired deep vein thrombosis / pulmonary embolism
POA (PRESENT ON ADMISSION)

Pre-Existing Baseline Path

A clinical diagnosis that was already established or clinically evident at the moment of inpatient intake (Elapsed hours = 0.0).

  • Pressure sore already documented by ambulance EMTs or ER triage
  • Pneumonia presenting with fever upon arrival at emergency desk
  • Chronic ulcerations from longstanding diabetic neuropathy
  • Legal outcome: Zero liability to the hospital; fully reimbursable

04. The 5-Pillar Mathematical Scoring Engine

How every claim receives an auditable, deterministic score from 0 to 100 based on five clinical criteria:

Pillar Evaluation Domain Max Pts Underlying Logic & Clinical Triggers
Component A Diagnosis Code Transparency 20 pts Bands A1–A6. Evaluates what the ICD-10 descriptor confesses. E.g., T81.4 explicitly names a care event (20 pts); T83.5 names a catheter device (16 pts); L89.152 is on complication surveillance list (6 pts).
Component B Timing & Inferred POA 20 pts Calculates elapsed hours ($\Delta t$). $\ge 48$ hours after admission = 20 pts (clear in-stay onset). $24–48$ hours = 12 pts. Hour 0.0 (present upon admission line) = 0 pts (Clean POA).
Component C Clinical Relatedness to Admission 20 pts Tests if complication is an expected natural progression. Real-data guarantee: Until approved AI model or certified relationship map is connected, returns UNKNOWN (10 pts) to prevent treating unknown as unrelated.
Component D Unplanned Rescue Intervention 25 pts Intervention Ladder: Return to theatre (25 pts), High-acuity rescue / ICU (20 pts), Unplanned operation (14 pts). Plus cumulative modifiers for rescue drugs (+3 pts), no pre-approval (+3 pts), or abnormal labs (+2 pts).
Component E Longitudinal Provider Linkage 15 pts 90-day patient lookback. Related surgery at same facility $\le 30$ days = 15 pts. Same facility 31–90 days = 9 pts. Different provider = 3 pts (routes liability to previous hospital). Clean history = 0 pts.

05. Fairness Suppressors & Safeguards

Mathematical ceilings that protect healthcare facilities handling high-acuity or complex referral cases.

🛡️ Recognized Progression Cap (Max 30)

If clinical documentation confirms that the patient's deterioration was an unavoidable biological progression (e.g., severe multi-organ failure or palliative care), the total score is hard-capped at 30 points to prevent unfair hospital penalty.

🏥 Different Provider Cap (Max 15)

If an admitted patient presents with complications arising from a surgery performed at another hospital, the receiving hospital's score is capped at 15 points, routing the investigation to the originating facility.
